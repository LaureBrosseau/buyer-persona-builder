# Maria Alvarez — Fleet Operations Manager

## Example Titles
- Fleet Operations Manager
- Fleet Manager
- Director of Fleet Operations
- Transportation Operations Manager
- Vehicle Operations Supervisor

## Summary
Maria manages 150-400 vehicles for a mid-sized logistics or delivery company (150-800 employees). She owns vehicle maintenance scheduling, cost control, and on-time delivery metrics. Most of her day is reactive: handling breakdown calls, expediting repairs, managing relationships with 3-5 maintenance vendors. She's measured on fleet uptime, maintenance spend per mile, and delivery performance. Her team is small (usually 2-4 people) and stretched.

## Wants, Needs and Desires
- Reduce emergency breakdowns and the resulting overtime and expedite fees
- Have a single view of vehicle health and maintenance status instead of checking three systems
- Spend less time firefighting and more time on prevention and cost optimization
- Get buy-in from leadership on budget for new tools with a clear ROI
- Make maintenance data-driven instead of relying on gut feel and vendor relationships

## Challenges
- Most work is reactive — things break, then she fixes them, rather than preventing breaks
- Maintenance data lives in separate systems (ERP, telematics, email, spreadsheets) with no integration
- Team is too small to adopt new processes without significant disruption
- Leadership questions every new tool purchase; needs strong ROI story to move budget
- High driver turnover due to vehicle reliability issues affects morale and retention

## Tasks and Responsibilities
- Schedule preventive maintenance for 150-400 vehicles, balancing cost and uptime
- Monitor daily vehicle availability and resolve breakdown situations
- Negotiate contracts and SLAs with maintenance vendors
- Report monthly fleet metrics (uptime, cost, safety) to VP of Operations or COO
- Train and manage 2-4 coordinators or dispatchers
- Manage a tight maintenance budget with limited flexibility for new tools
