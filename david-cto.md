# David Kim — Chief Technology Officer

## Example Titles
- Chief Technology Officer
- VP of Engineering
- Head of Technology
- Technical Co-Founder
- VP of Product & Engineering

## Summary
David leads engineering and technical strategy for a Series B-D SaaS company (80-400 employees). He owns architecture decisions, technical hiring, and vendor evaluation for anything touching the engineering stack. He's measured on system reliability, engineering velocity, and technical debt reduction. He splits his time between hands-on technical review, managing 3-8 engineering leads, and defending the technical roadmap to the executive team and board.

## Wants, Needs and Desires
- Reduce time engineers spend on maintenance and firefighting versus building new features
- Adopt tools that integrate cleanly with the existing stack, not ones requiring a rewrite
- Prove technical decisions with data, not just intuition, when justifying spend to the CEO or board
- Build a team and system that scales without heroics or burnout
- Stay credible with engineers by not forcing tools that create more work than they save

## Challenges
- Balancing technical debt paydown against pressure to ship new features fast
- Evaluating vendor claims skeptically — has been burned by tools that under-delivered
- Justifying infrastructure or tooling spend to non-technical executives and the board
- Keeping senior engineers engaged and retained in a competitive hiring market
- Security and compliance requirements growing faster than the team built to handle them

## Tasks and Responsibilities
- Set technical architecture and long-term engineering roadmap
- Evaluate and approve new tools, platforms, and vendors for the engineering org
- Manage and mentor engineering leads across 3-8 teams
- Report on system reliability, velocity, and technical risk to the executive team
- Own security, compliance, and infrastructure decisions
- Balance hands-on technical work with organizational and people leadership
